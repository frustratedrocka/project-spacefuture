# 20 meters of humanoid weapon

Why are mobile suits even a thing:

In a word, versatility. They tie (and occasionally win) most tradeoffs vs anything else in their roles, and they fill *most* major combat armor roles. They've got less raw speed than a strike fighter or bomber, but are *much* more agile, their firing arcs aren't restricted by thrust vectors, and are easier to store more of in less hangar space. They're much bigger and less armor-efficient than tanks, but pack much more powerful weaponry and are effectively all-terrain. And, most importantly, the same mobile suit can usually handle space and groundbound operations just fine, and even change roles within the same engagement, even if they're often designed with one specialty in mind.

The biggest notable exceptions are transports and terrestrial aircraft. "Box with engines" is still unchallenged for getting lots of people and gear from point A to point B, and mobile suits don't have the thrust-to-weight ratio (and certainly not the aerodynamics) needed to fly in atmosphere and gravity, even weaker Martian gravity.

# Safety First?
Somewhat surprisingly, mobile suits in the emergent century actually do have ejection systems to aid pilot survival. A standard cockpit is a self-contained, somewhat pill-shaped, and fairly cramped capsule. In an emergency, as a last ditch effort, the cockpit can launch itself clear of the suit. 

>[!aside|s-t]+ Tech Marches On
>Newer cockpits, like the ones used in the [[Database/Mobile Suits/AP-92 Rejunot\|AP-92 Rejunot]] and now-destroyed [[Database/Mobile Suits/EW-14 Gundam Makhairos\|EW-14 Gundam Makhairos]], are spherical and project a 360 view of their surroundings instead of relying on an incomplete array of discrete monitors. This is visually stunning, incredibly helpful in battle, and nearly impossible to retrofit onto older ground-war era suits, so anyone still using one of those suits who's still attached to it is stuck with the older version.

The cockpit's survival systems include stabilization thrusters to make sure you can eventually slow down enough to be recovered and mitigate excessive spinning, a set of extremely photoreflective airbags, an emergency air supply, a signal beacon, and explosive bolts and charges meant to launch you out the blown-open back of your MS at speed. Your [[Database/Glossary/Normal Suit\|Normal Suit]] can handle general "being exposed to the vacuum of space" duties for a little while (as occurred in [[Session Notes/Episode 02\|Episode 02]]'s spacewalks); the pod's main function is to keep you from getting splattered during the ejection process, throw you as far from the battle as possible, give you extra oxygen so you can live longer if things go sideways, and light up an impossible-to-miss "I AM A LIFE POD PLEASE COME SAVE MY ASS" sign. 

An ejection is not subtle, by design. You can secure the beacon afterwards if you don't want the enemy to know where you're landing, but there's rarely any way to hide that an ejection has happened. That said, against foes that have established themselves as not taking prisoners, you can switch that default channel to secured or completely off.

**NOBODY SHOOTS AT LIFE PODS. BECAUSE DOING SO IS *DUMB*.*** As with faking surrender, there are war crimes that will get you tried and shot by the enemy *after* the war, and there are war crimes that will get you absolutely reamed out and possibly court-martialed by your own command *during* the war, and there is very little overlap between those two lists. Shooting life pods falls firmly in the second category. War is hell; that being said, it is marginally less hellish and strategically preferable for everyone involved if losing your mobile suit is not automatically a death sentence.

Now, if you *do* get taken prisoner and your captors don't have enough food available to hold you, that's a completely different and much more understandable thing. After the Ground War, everybody understands that sometimes them's the breaks. But this universe recognizes a meaningful difference between resource-driven triage-necessitated murder and heat-of-the-moment hors-de-combat murder.
